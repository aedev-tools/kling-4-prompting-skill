# Examples and focused revisions

These are original teaching prompts, not verified Kling renders. Durations are planning choices; select supported settings in the interface you use.

## Animate a portrait, 5 seconds

Brief: a person waiting at a bus stop notices someone approaching. Keep the camera still.

```text
Begin with the supplied portrait. The person shifts their eyes toward
the right edge of frame, then turns their head slightly in that direction.
Their shoulders relax and a small smile appears. The camera stays fixed
at eye level. A passing bus can be heard in the distance. No music or
subtitles.
```

## A scene from scratch, 8 seconds

Brief: a tiny robot opens a bakery for the first time.

```text
Early morning inside a small bakery. A palm-sized tin robot stands
on a stool behind the counter, wearing a flour-dusted apron. It reaches
up with both hands, flips the door sign to OPEN, then steps back to
inspect it. A slow camera push toward the door finishes on the robot
looking out through the glass. Low sunlight crosses the wooden floor.
A soft metal footstep and the door bell ringing. No background music.
```

## Two people speaking, 10 seconds

Brief: a quiet repair-shop exchange, exactly these two lines.

```text
A repair shop after closing. Mara stands beside a bicycle on a work
stand; Eli sits at the bench. One medium two-shot, the camera fixed.
Mara looks at the loose wheel and says quietly, "You kept it?"
Eli waits a beat, looks up, and replies, "I said I'd fix it."
Mara rests her hand on the saddle. A desk fan hums beside the tools.
No subtitles or music.
```

Keep the user's words when adapting. Allow sufficient speaking time rather than forcing long dialogue into this duration.

## A deliberately short prompt

```text
A paper boat crosses a shallow puddle. The camera stays at water level
as the boat drifts past, leaving small ripples behind it.
```

A simple idea does not need a full storyboard or a lens list.

## Troubleshooting

| Result | First revision to try |
| --- | --- |
| Camera circles an object | Specify the fixed viewpoint and let the object rotate |
| An image changes too much | Remove invented appearance details; describe the movement |
| A new person appears | Identify who occupies the shot and what each person does |
| A sequence feels rushed | Reduce action or allow more time |
| Dialogue goes to the wrong person | Tie each line to a named speaker and visible action |
| An edit changes the whole room | Narrow the requested change and identify the retained room layout |
| Music obscures speech | Specify the desired sound bed and exclude background music if unwanted |

Change one relevant part at a time when comparing results. A better-specified prompt can still produce a failed generation.
