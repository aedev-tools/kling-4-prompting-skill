# Shots and keyframes

Read for a storyboard, several shots, or images tied to moments in time.

## Pick the control the user needs

Separate a desired look from a required visual state at a particular time. Kling documents Omni Reference and Keyframes as separate entry points; Keyframes accepts images rather than video references. Do not combine their upload controls. See [sources](sources.md).

If the request needs motion from a video and exact intermediate frames, explain the tradeoff and ask which matters most, or propose separate tasks. Do not silently discard one requirement. Match the actual selected interface.

## Plan a sequence

List each shot with its duration, framing, action, and transition. Make the intervals cover the requested duration without accidental gaps or overlaps. Carry identity, props, screen direction, and light sources across cuts unless the story changes them.

Keep a continuous take continuous. A reframing within a shot is not automatically a cut. Distinguish camera motion, a subject moving through the frame, and an edit between viewpoints.

Original teaching example, 12-second text-to-video:

```text
A small roadside coffee stand just after sunrise. The same blue
ceramic cup and wooden counter appear throughout.

Shot 1, 0-4 seconds: Tight overhead view of coffee beans spilling
from a paper bag onto the counter. Morning light enters from the left.

Shot 2, 4-8 seconds: Cut to a close side view. A hand pours coffee
into the blue cup. Steam rises into the light. The camera holds still.

Shot 3, 8-12 seconds: Cut wider. The customer takes the cup from
the counter, leaving the open landscape visible behind it.

Audio: beans tapping wood, coffee pouring, a distant bird.
No music or subtitles.
```

## Plan keyframes

Confirm which image goes at each time before writing the transitions. Respect the first and last states. A middle frame is a story constraint, not an extra mood board image. If timestamps are unavailable in the provider, describe the intended sequence and explain which timing remains approximate.

Write the process between states. Consider physical distance, direction, occlusion, and how many actions must happen. Do not promise that identical endpoints alone produce a seamless loop; the motion and lighting also need to join.

For a pottery sequence, a plausible plan might move from a rough clay cylinder to an open bowl, then finish on hands lifting away. Use reference photos only when supplied. An absent middle photo is a proposed asset to prepare, never an invented upload.

When exact timing or a frame-perfect match matters, recommend checking the generated clip and finishing in an editor. Prompt instructions express intent; they do not certify the output.
